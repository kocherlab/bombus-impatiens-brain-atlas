A bumble bee brain, cell by cell.

1,300,192 cells from the brains of a queen and a worker of the common eastern bumble bee, each typed and placed back where it was.

Mushroom bodies

Centres of learning and memory. Each Kenyon cell compartment is named for the type that dominates it: Large-type non-compact Small-type inner compact Class II outer compact

Large-type subclusters

Within the Large-type compartment, three subclusters: S1 S2 S3

Optic lobes

Where what the compound eyes see is processed: Lamina Medulla Lobula

One section

The brain was cut into serial 10 µm sections, each imaged cell by cell, then aligned back into one volume.

The bee

A bumble bee colony lasts one season. A queen who mated the summer before spends the winter alone underground. In spring she founds a nest, builds a ball of pollen and rears the first brood herself.

Those first offspring are workers: daughters who forage and tend the nest while the queen stays in to lay eggs. Late in the season the colony raises new queens and males. The new queens mate and overwinter, and the old colony dies.

Queens and workers share a genome but lead very different lives. This atlas maps the brain of one queen and one worker from the same colony.

One of eachWith one brain per caste, a difference between them describes two individuals. It is not a tested effect of caste.

Scale

~3mm

across a bumble bee brain, from one optic lobe to the other. Brains scale with body size: in this atlas the worker’s measures 3.0 mm and the queen’s 3.6 mm, and every cell here fits inside one of the two.
